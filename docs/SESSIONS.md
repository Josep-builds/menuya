## What the evidence says (Adversary read)

1. **The waste exists, but $60 a week of saving is still unproven.** Reported waste is $90–130 a week, so the money is there. To save $60, though, U1 would have to cut about 46% of his waste and U2 about 67%. Both figures are recall-based estimates from one week.
2. **Only part of their delivery spending is a problem MenúYA can solve.** Of 20 orders, 9 were "no planeé" or "no tuve tiempo", which a plan can address. At least 4 were alcohol or cigarettes, which no meal plan touches, and others were antojo. The 10:1 can only count the 9.
3. **Peak need confirmed: days 12–15 and deadline weeks.** U2 named days 12–15 unprompted, and U1 named project deadlines. This supports the "rescue my remaining week" and "no time" modes over a Sunday 7-day plan (matches synthetic S3/S5).
4. **Both said "yes, if it proves it saves."** That's the polite yes the Crystal Ball provocation warns about, and the same reaction as synthetic S1. It means showing proof of the saving inside the app (the Contador de Ahorro) comes before any payment.
5. **New signal: shared households.** U2 shares groceries and would split the price with his roommate. Worth testing a household price (one plan, two people) later.
6. **Bulk buying hurts single people.** Both buy at Costco, where pack sizes are too big for one or two people, and both threw out tortillas. The shopping list should account for package size.
